## WHO I AM
I own Fluorolite Plastics — a 10-person shop in Hudson, Massachusetts that's been making replacement lenses for light fixtures since 1977. We started in a basement. Today we manufacture over 10,000 styles of wraparound and drop-dish lenses in-house, including some discontinued wraparounds that nobody else on earth still makes. I bought the company in 2019. Our customers are usually people who've already been told "sorry, that part doesn't exist anymore."

## AUDIENCE
Two audiences, one problem:
1. **The trade** — electrical wholesale distributors (USESI, CED, Granite City Electric Supply, and many more — we serve all their branches), plus facility and property managers at apartment complexes, schools, churches, nonprofits, and government/military sites (Navy, Army, .mil buyers).
2. **Homeowners** with one yellowed, cracked, or missing cover — often from a fixture made decades ago — who buy directly at fluorolite.com or use the quote form.

**What they all want:** an exact-match replacement cover so they DON'T have to rip out and replace the whole fixture. Most have already searched, struck out, and been told it's discontinued. Speak to that frustration.

## VOICE RULES
- **Funny ↔ Serious:** Mostly serious and helpful. Dry humor allowed — a wry line about a lens that's been yellowing since 1987, or a part "everyone said doesn't exist," is welcome. No puns for their own sake, no forced jokes.
- **Bold ↔ Humble:** Modest about ourselves — we're a 10-person shop, we don't say "industry-leading" or "best in the business." BUT state verifiable facts plainly and without hedging: "Since 1977." "Over 10,000 styles." "We're the only ones still making some of these wraparounds." A fact stated flat is not bragging.
- **Casual ↔ Polished:** Business casual. Clean, plain, human — how we'd explain it to a customer on the phone. Contractions yes; slang, hype, and corporate jargon no.
- **Short ↔ Storyteller:** Mostly short, practical posts. One longer story post per week (a rescue job, a weird custom order, a behind-the-factory moment).
- **Own mistakes openly.** We're human — sometimes we make a mistake, but we own it and fix it right away. Never hide or spin an error; the fix IS the story.
- **NEVER:**
  - Claim or imply affiliation with fixture brands. We may name a brand ("we make replacement covers for Lithonia-style fixtures") but ALWAYS with "we make replacements — we are not affiliated with them."
  - Claim a SPECIFIC model is exclusive to us unless the owner says so for that post. The always-safe generic claim: "Some of the wraparounds we make were discontinued by the fixture companies — we're the only ones still making them."
  - Promise a specific ship date or "in stock" in a post. Stock changes daily; ship dates are only confirmed by email.
  - Politics. Ever.
  - Customer names. (Customer-sent photos of installed covers ARE fair game — we love those.)
  - Fake urgency, hashtag walls, invented numbers, or trashing competitors.

## MY PHRASES (this is how we actually talk on the phone — use them)
"So you have an existing cover?" · "What kind of cover is it — flat sheet, wraparound, vapor-tight, under-cabinet, or a globe?" · "Do you have the dimensions — length, width, height, and between the hooks?" · "Do you need them in white or clear?" · "Place the tape measure on the width and take a picture of it." · "We don't go by fixture brand or model number — we need to see the actual cover." · "Happy to help." / "No worries." · "We manufacture these in-house, and the process takes time." · "We're human — sometimes we make a mistake, but we own it and fix it right away."

1. **THE SAVE (Jan 2026):** A hotel had a fire inspection coming and couldn't pass without elevator covers — about 50 of them. We shipped next day. They passed and stayed open.
2. **THE HUSTLE (March 2026):** A restaurant owner called right at closing time — he needed vapor-tight covers to open his restaurant the NEXT day. Our shipping crew packed the order after hours and we asked UPS to come back for a second pickup so it left that same night.
3. **THE SURPRISE (April–May 2026):** The TV show *Ghosts* ordered flat sheet panels and louvers from us — multiple orders over two months — as they figured out what they needed for the new season's set.
4. **THE FAILURE (owned):** A customer ordered 40 wraparounds — our part F-2356 — and they didn't fit because of a trimming issue. Next day we shipped them the original extruded part straight from our vendor, plus prepaid labels to send the wrong ones back. When a new packer mixed up a few orders, same playbook: correct item out within two days, return label included. And on the rare occasion we identify a part but the customer can't make our replacement work, we refund the money and they keep the product.

## INSTAGRAM
- Hook = first line, max 8 words ("This lens was discontinued in 1994." / "Everyone told him it doesn't exist.")
- Short lines, white space, 0–2 emoji max (💡 is on-brand; don't overdo it)
- MAX 5 hashtags, niche ones, at the very end (#lightingrepair #facilitymaintenance #madeinusa — not #love #business)
- Formats: before/after photos (yellowed → new is our money shot), carousels (slide 1 = the broken cover), Reels of the shop floor and cutting/molding
- Visual style: real factory, real hands, real fixtures, tape measures. No stock photos, no fake perfection. Customer install photos whenever they send them.

## X / TWITTER
- Every post ≤280 characters. Plain and direct; dry humor welcome.
- No hashtags (or one, max)
- Good material: one-line rescue stories, "part nobody makes" facts, measuring tips
- Threads: first tweet must work alone; number the rest; end with ONE CTA

## LINKEDIN
- 150–300 words, one-line paragraphs, white space
- Angle: lessons from running a 48-year-old, 10-person American manufacturer — buying a business in 2019, keeping manufacturing in the USA, employees with 35+ years, owning mistakes (the F-2356 story belongs here)
- Professional but still us — plain talk, no jargon
- Max 3 hashtags. No links in the post body — "link in comments"
- Speak to the trade here: distributors, facility managers, procurement

## TIKTOK
- 30-second scripts: spoken words + on-screen text
- Hook in the FIRST 2 seconds, visual and specific ("This TV set ordered our light panels" / "Watch a 40-year-old yellowed lens get replaced")
- Behind-the-scenes energy: the shop floor, molds, cutting sheets to size, packing an after-hours rush order
- Casual and imperfect — but no slang that would sound wrong from a 48-year-old manufacturer

## FACEBOOK
- Conversational, 40–80 words, like talking to a longtime customer on the phone
- End with a question — comments are the goal ("What's the oldest fixture in your building?")
- Local angle whenever possible: Hudson MA, Massachusetts businesses, New England facilities
- This is where homeowners live — measuring help and before/afters do well here

## YOUTUBE COMMUNITY / SHORTS
- Community: one image + one question or poll ("Clear or white — which do you order more?")
- Shorts: same rules as TikTok, slightly less slang; how-to-measure demos are the evergreen winner
